# Content positioning

Source: a third-party coaching checklist, "Top 5 YouTube Algorithm & Positioning Mistakes to Avoid" (a lead magnet from a YouTube strategy coach), rewritten here in our own words and adapted for B2B on LinkedIn. It was written for YouTube. The rules hold for any feed where a stranger decides in two seconds. Our buyers are CCOs, MLROs, GCs, fund principals and treasurers (see `bd-content-strategy.md`).

## The five mistakes (test every asset)

1. **Noticeboard.** Announcing us (badges, launches, team news, funding) instead of answering a problem the buyer has. Fix: open on the buyer's problem. Put our news in the third line as the reason to trust.
2. **Disguised pitch.** Every piece sells. Fix: the reader gets something usable even if they never call us (a checklist, a test, a number to compare).
3. **Jargon and internal names.** Titles built on acronyms, report names or our own terms. Fix: use the words a buyer would type or say. Spell out the acronym on first use. Say what it means for them.
4. **Assumed reputation.** New readers don't know our titles. Fix: one concrete reason to trust within the title, the image, or the first 2 to 3 lines on LinkedIn (the part visible before "see more") or the first 30 seconds of video. Use a number or a named role, not an adjective.
5. **Packaging after production.** Writing first, titling last. Fix: write three titles (and the cover or thumbnail text) before drafting. Pick one. If time allows, test on about 5 people in the target roles.

## Before drafting: pick the angle

Choose one, state it in a sentence, then write:
- **Credential:** a role or past employer, with numbers.
- **Result:** something that happened, with numbers.
- **Contrarian view:** where the experience contradicts standard advice.
- **Access:** data, people or processes most readers can't see.

Add our spin to a topic that already has attention: our own experience, a real client number, or a framework. Don't invent any of these. Where we hold none, leave an `[ASK]` slot.

## Formats that work for us

- **Teardown or audit:** take one regulator document, read it live, give the reader the questions it raises. This is our default (it shows judgement and needs only a public source).
- **Full guide:** one complete checklist or walkthrough.
- **I've done it:** only with a real, sourced result.
- **Experiment:** only with real data.
- **Origin story, asymmetric outcome ("X in Y minutes"), hero content:** use rarely. They need personal stakes or borrowed fame we may not have.

Prefer a repeating show over one-offs: same structure, new source each week.

## Hook, in order

1. Confirm the promise (what the reader gets).
2. One concrete reason to trust.
3. Start delivering. No warm-up, no "in today's landscape".

## Funnel

End with one next step that fits the metrics in `bd-content-strategy.md` (inbound DM, brief sign-up, discovery call). Offer a free, usable asset first. Never promise an asset that doesn't exist: confirm it exists or mark `[CONFIRM]`.

## Checklist to run on every asset

- [ ] Three title options written before the draft, one picked
- [ ] Opens on the buyer's problem, not on us
- [ ] A buyer could type the title into a search box
- [ ] Acronyms spelled out on first use
- [ ] One concrete reason to trust in the first 2 to 3 lines or 30 seconds
- [ ] Useful with no sale attached
- [ ] One next step, and its asset exists
- [ ] Every fact traces to a primary source, or carries `[ASK]` or `[VERIFY]`
- [ ] Voice pass done (Sarah: `sarah-voice-profile` plus `anti-ai-writing-style`; Anwar: `_local/anwar-social-voice.md`)
